
### 2026-09-07 — The severity pyramid was inverted because the thresholds were graded on their own homework
**What happened:** After retraining on the cleaned archive, the published alert counts still read HIGH 25, CRITICAL 32 — more of the most severe tier than the tier below it, which cannot be right for thresholds meant to fire at 4% and 0.5%.
**What we tried first:** Assumed the escalation rules were promoting too aggressively, since corroboration can lift a reading by a tier. They were not: the *base* bands, before any escalation, were already inverted at 81 HIGH against 158 CRITICAL.
**What we learned:** The thresholds were quantiles of the residuals of the fitted model **against its own training rows**. A boosted tree fits its training data closely, so those residuals are far tighter than the errors the model actually makes, and two things followed. The whole ladder sat far too low — at P05, 10.9% of readings cleared a bar intended to be exceeded 0.5% of the time, a twenty-fold error. And the top of the distribution compressed: P05's 96th and 99.5th percentiles landed at 0.00809 and 0.00862, making the HIGH band 0.0005 wide, so nearly everything that reached HIGH sailed past it into CRITICAL. We had already fixed this symptom once by switching from Gaussian z-thresholds to empirical quantiles, and it came straight back, because the distribution being quantiled was the wrong one. The anomaly detector had been calibrating on validation data all along; the baseline was the one component still grading its own homework.
**What we changed:** Thresholds and the robust spread now come from **out-of-fold** residuals — rolling-origin folds over the training window, each fitting on the past and predicting the next block, so no threshold is set from a prediction the model already knew the answer to. Validation stays free for the anomaly threshold; the test period is still untouched. Base bands became WATCH 83, ELEVATED 23, HIGH 7, CRITICAL 4, and the CRITICAL bar is now cleared on 0.0–0.4% of readings as designed. Two smaller things fell out of it: an early fold is short enough that a feature can be constant within it, which HistGradientBoosting cannot bin at all, so each fold keeps only the columns that vary in its own rows; and `train.yml` never committed `EVALUATION.md`, so the human-readable report had been describing models that no longer existed.
**What fixing it exposed:** With honest thresholds, the alert rate over the training period fell to 0.2% against 17.6% on unseen data. The thresholds were right; the *history* was the lie — the model recalls rows it was fitted on, so plotting its residuals as history showed nine calm years and alerts starting the month training stopped, an artifact of the schedule presented as something the river did. The fold predictions are now kept and the dashboard shows, for each historical date, what the system would have said at the time: 15.7% / 18.8% / 17.6% across train, validation and test, a pyramid in each. The earliest ~20% of each station's training rows have no fold prediction — nothing precedes them to fit on — and still use the in-sample value. **The lesson worth writing up:** a threshold is a model output, and every rule we apply to model evaluation applies to it. We knew not to evaluate on training data and did it anyway, one layer down, where it did not look like evaluation.
